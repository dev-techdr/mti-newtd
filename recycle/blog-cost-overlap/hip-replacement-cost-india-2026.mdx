A primary hip replacement in India is listed at USD 5,500–8,000 in the 2026 TechDr guide, for surgery, a standard implant, the hospital stay and ordinary medicines. Most people stay 4–7 days in hospital and about 2–3 weeks in India. The same guide puts a hip at USD 40,000–55,000 in the USA and USD 18,000–27,000 in the UK. These are planning ranges, not a quote for your hip.

</section>

## How much does a hip replacement cost in India in 2026?

The useful figure is the one on a letter that names the implant, the bearing surface, the number of nights, and the price of an extra night. A website band cannot do that. TechDr asks partner hospitals for that letter before you pay a deposit or book a non-refundable flight.

Revision surgery, infection, bone graft, and a dual-mobility cup sit outside the USD 5,500–8,000 band. So does a second hip. If both hips are worn, ask for two lines, not a slogan that says “both joints”.

## What does a standard hip package include?

A primary package is built for one worn hip in a person who is fit for surgery. It usually covers the surgeon and anaesthesia team, the theatre, a standard socket and stem, a ward bed for the planned stay, routine tests, routine medicines in hospital, and the first walking sessions on the ward. Airport pickup and a hotel for a companion belong in the letter only if they are written there.

Ask whether the bearing is metal-on-plastic, ceramic-on-plastic, or ceramic-on-ceramic. There is no single right surface for every age. An unnamed “imported implant” is not a specification.

## What is usually excluded?

Flights, the visa, travel insurance, and a companion’s food are outside the surgery line. So is treatment of a heart or kidney problem found on the pre-operative tests, a blood transfusion if it is capped, and physiotherapy after you leave India. A raised toilet seat and a walking frame for home are cheap and easy to forget. Buy or borrow them before you fly home.

## How long do you stay, and when can you fly?

An uncomplicated primary hip often means 4–7 nights in hospital. Plan 2–3 weeks in India so the wound can be checked and a clinician can say whether a long flight is reasonable. Swelling, a fever, a draining wound, or a swollen calf is a reason to stay.

The surgical approach (anterior, lateral, or posterior) changes the sitting precautions in the first weeks. It does not change the need for a written fitness-to-fly note. A cheap fare on day ten is not medical clearance.

## Who is a reasonable candidate to travel?

People who travel usually have groin or thigh pain that limits walking and sleep after a proper trial of medicines and physiotherapy, plus an X-ray that matches that story. A film alone is not a reason to fly. A fracture that needs urgent fixation belongs in the nearest capable hospital, not on a planned medical trip.

Tell the team about previous clots, anaemia, and any earlier hip operation. Those facts change the implant and the stay. They are not automatic refusals.

## What should you send before a quote?

Send a short history, medicines, allergies, and a pelvis X-ray that shows both hips. Add an MRI if avascular necrosis has been mentioned. Name the country you fly from and whether someone will travel with you. Photos of an X-ray on a phone screen are a start. The hospital will want the file before a final plan.

## How should you read the written quote?

Check one hip or two, the implant name, the bearing, the ward nights, the daily rate after that cap, and whether ICU is included. Then check who you pay. A hospital invoice in the hospital’s name is clearer than a transfer to a personal account.
