A medical tourism package is a written list, not a slogan. It should state the procedure, the device or implant, the ward nights, the intensive-care cap, the standard tests and medicines, and the exclusions. Airport pickup and a hotel are extras. If the implant, the ICU days, and the exclusions are missing, you do not have a package. You have a number that can change after you land.

</section>

## What does “package” mean here?

Hospitals and coordinators use the word for two different things. One is the clinical bundle: the operation and the hospital care around it. The other is the trip bundle: visa letter, airport pickup, hotel, interpreter, and a local SIM. TechDr can arrange both. They should be priced as separate lines so a hotel upgrade is not confused with a better implant. This article is about the clinical bundle, because that is where people are surprised.

A package is not a fixed medical outcome. It is a fixed list of services for a stated price, with rules for what happens outside the list.

## Which clinical lines should always be visible?

The procedure name, specific enough to match your reports. The professional fees for surgery and anaesthesia, or a statement that they are inside the total. The operating theatre. The implant, lens, stent, valve, or other device, named or honestly described as a category that will be named before admission. The number of ward nights. The number of ICU or high-dependency nights included, even if that number is zero because the operation rarely needs them. Routine tests. Routine medicines during the stay. The first physiotherapy, if the operation needs it. Discharge papers in English.

If a line is “included if needed”, ask for the cap. Unlimited inclusion sounds generous and is often undefined.

## Which trip lines are optional?

Airport pickup, a hotel before admission, a hotel after discharge, an extra bed for a companion, an interpreter, a SIM, and help with currency. These are worth buying if the price is clear. They do not make the surgery safer by themselves. A coordinator who talks only about the hotel and not about the ICU cap has inverted the package.

Food for a companion, international calls, and sightseeing are not medical costs. Keep them out of the clinical quote so you can see the medicine.

## What is commonly excluded?

Your flights, visas, and insurance. Treatment of illnesses that are not the reason for the trip. A more expensive device than the one named. Extra nights above the cap. A return to theatre. Dialysis, chemotherapy, or a long course of antibiotics that was not part of the original plan. Care in another hospital if you discharge yourself. Medicines after the first discharge supply. A complication that appears after you fly home is almost always outside the Indian hospital package, which is why your doctor at home must be part of the plan.

Read the exclusion list twice. It is the real contract.

## How should complications be priced?

Ask the question in plain language. If I bleed tonight and return to theatre, is that inside this fee, capped, or a new invoice? If I need three extra ICU days, what is the daily rate? If the stent count goes from one to three because of the angiogram, how does the price move? Honest units answer with numbers. Units that say “we will take care of you” are being kind and unclear at the same time. You can thank them and still ask for the number.

A small buffer in your own budget is still necessary. No package can predict every day.

## How do you compare two packages?

Put them in the same rows. If one includes the lens and the other bills the lens separately, add the lens before you decide the second hospital is more expensive. If one includes seven ward nights and the other includes four, they are not the same product. If one names the implant and the other says “imported”, they are not the same product either.

Ignore gifts. A free city tour does not belong in the comparison. Neither does a celebrity photograph.

## What should you pay, and when?

Pay a hospital or a company you can identify, against an invoice that matches the quote. A small deposit can be reasonable if the refund rule covers visa refusal and medical unsuitability. A large payment to a personal account, before a visa, is not a package. It is a risk. Keep receipts. Ask which currency the hospital will charge and whether a card payment adds a fee.

## What documents prove what you bought?

The quote, the consent, the final bill, the operation note, and the device stickers. They should tell the same story. If the bill lists a different implant from the quote, ask before you leave the billing desk. Take photos. Your insurer at home, if you hope to claim anything, will ask for this paper. Many insurers will not reimburse planned care abroad. Know that before you rely on a claim.

## A short checklist before you pay

Procedure matches the reports. Device named or scheduled to be named. Ward nights and extra-night price printed. ICU cap printed. Exclusions printed. Companion costs separated. Refund rule printed. Night contact printed. Doctor at home identified. If any row is empty, wait.

## How a knee package and a heart package differ

Use the same headings and expect different contents. A single knee listed around USD 4,500–7,000 should name the implant, about 5–7 ward nights, and physiotherapy. Intensive care may be a zero-day cap with a daily price, because many primary knees never need it. A bypass listed around USD 5,000–8,500 should name the planned grafts, a real ICU allowance, and the medicines you must not stop after a related stent. A cataract at USD 600–1,500 per eye should name the lens. A liver transplant at USD 28,000–40,000 should separate the recipient operation from the donor’s evaluation. Copying one package shape onto every operation is how people buy a joint-replacement bundle for a heart operation.

## What “no hidden fees” can honestly mean

It can mean the letter lists every fee the hospital already knows how to charge. It cannot mean a complication is free. TechDr uses “no hidden fees” for the practice of printing exclusions and extra-day prices before you pay, not for a promise that biology will stay inside the average. If a coordinator waves the phrase instead of the list, ask for the list again.

## After you accept

Keep the quote with your passport. On admission, ask the billing desk to confirm the package code matches that PDF. Small discrepancies are easier to fix before the anaesthetic than on the morning you want to fly. Your companion should know the night number and the extra-day price so you do not have to negotiate from a recovery bed.
