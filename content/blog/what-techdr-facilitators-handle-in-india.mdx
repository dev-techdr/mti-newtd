<section id="quick-answer" aria-label="Quick answer">

A TechDr facilitator coordinates planned treatment in India. They collect your reports, ask a partner hospital for a written plan and an itemised estimate, and, after you accept, arrange the invitation letter, airport pickup, a hotel near that campus, and the records you carry home. Senior specialists review the file. Independent teams at JCI and NABH-accredited partner hospitals do the treatment. TechDr is not the surgeon.

</section>

## Who do you actually talk to?

You talk to a case manager. That person checks whether the file is complete, chases the missing scan, and puts the quote in writing. The clinical view comes from senior specialists, described on the [about page](/about) by role and by about 15–30 or more years of experience. The website does not publish their names or the hospital names. Ask for the role of the person who will operate. A famous name in a chat is not that answer.

Patients sometimes call this person a consultant and sometimes a facilitator. The [consultant guide](/blog/medical-tourism-consultant-india) separates the coordinator from the specialist who reads the scans. Only the second can form a clinical view, and even that view is an opinion, not a booking.

## What arrives before anyone asks you to fly?

A written plan that names the operation and the exclusions. An itemised estimate in USD, labelled as indicative until the hospital letter replaces it. A statement of what a deposit covers if the visa is refused or the surgeon says you are not fit. The party you pay.

The clock TechDr states is about 48 hours after a usable file, not after a message that says “need the best hospital.” What to send is in the [document guide](/blog/documents-for-a-treatment-quote). A photo of a screen can start the conversation. It is not a final plan. No hospital is booked before you accept. The [how it works page](/how-it-works) is those steps in order.

## What happens after you accept the plan?

The partner hospital can issue an invitation letter for your visa application. TechDr does not stamp a passport. Read the [visa guide](/blog/india-e-medical-visa-guide) and confirm the current rules on the official Indian visa portal before you buy tickets.

You send the flight number. Pickup is arranged against that number. The hotel should be the one near the campus in the quote, not a cheaper room across the city. A companion’s practical questions are part of the job. Heart, brain, and transplant trips need that companion, and the attendant visa is a separate application. The [companion guide](/blog/companion-for-surgery-abroad) is the detail.

Before you leave, the papers that belong in the cabin bag are the operation note, implant stickers, the medicine list, and the discharge summary. The [records guide](/blog/records-to-carry-home-after-surgery) is the list. Video follow-up after you land is coordination with the treating team’s instructions. It does not replace the doctor who sees you at home.

## What stays outside the facilitator’s job?

Operating. Granting a visa. Overruling a transplant authorisation committee. Telling you to stop blood thinners, diabetes drugs, or antiplatelet tablets. Those instructions come from the treating team, in writing. Guaranteeing a bed on a date the hospital has not confirmed. Managing an emergency from another country.

TechDr also refuses paid organ donation, a guaranteed cure, a commercial flight for someone too unwell to travel, and surrogacy arrangements. A facilitator who sells those as packages is not doing this job. The same refusals are in the [facilitator guide](/blog/best-medical-tourism-facilitators-india).

You pay the hospital named on the invoice. You do not pay a personal account to hold a bed. Keep the quote and the legal name outside the chat app. The [payment guide](/blog/how-to-pay-a-hospital-in-india) is the rule.

## Which practical details should you expect in writing?

Coordinators can work in English, Arabic, French, Russian, Bengali, and Hindi. Ask which language the discharge summary will use, and ask for an interpreter in the consent conversation if you need one. Consent in a language you do not understand is not informed consent.

Ask who replies to a wound photo after you fly home, and what the quote excludes, including an extra ICU day. If the answer is only “we are the best facilitators in India,” you have not been given a plan. The [agency guide](/blog/best-medical-tourism-agency-india) uses the same five questions. Facilitator and agency are the words patients type. The job is the same if the letter is the same.
