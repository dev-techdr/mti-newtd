## Why do company names look the same in search?

Many sites use the same phrases: best medical tourism company in India, best facilitator, best agency, number one for foreigners. Search results reward the phrase. They do not audit the invoice. Two companies can both be honest. You still have to read the letter for your operation. A third can be a broker with a cheap headline and no hospital behind it. The checks below are how you tell them apart without trusting a badge.

## What belongs on the comparison page?

Write five lines for each company.

Legal name, address, and a tax identifier you can match to the invoice. TechDr publishes these on the [about page](/about): Gorityala Abhista Sai, trading as TechDr, brand Medical Tours India.

Who sends the clinical letter. It should be a hospital team, not a sales script. The operation, implant or device, nights, ICU cap, and exclusions should be visible. Use the [package guide](/blog/whats-included-in-a-medical-tourism-package).

Who you pay, and in which currency. The [payment guide](/blog/how-to-pay-a-hospital-in-india) says to pay the hospital named on the quote.

What happens if the visa is refused, the committee says no, or you are not fit. That sentence should exist before you transfer a deposit.

Who answers after you fly home, and how that person reaches the treating team. An agency that disappears at the airport has not finished the job.

## How do you use price in the comparison?

Put the Indian letter next to a home quote that counts the same items, then add flights, a companion, and medicines. The [cost comparison](/blog/medical-tourism-cost-calculator) is that sum. A company that is “cheapest” because the implant is missing is not cheaper. Knee packages in the 2026 TechDr guide sit around USD 4,500–7,000, and a standard bypass around USD 5,000–8,500. A figure far under those bands needs a written reason.

Insurance rarely makes the companies equal. Many travel policies exclude planned treatment. Read the [insurance guide](/blog/medical-travel-insurance-before-surgery) before you treat a policy as the safety net.

## Which differences are real, and which are decoration?

Real: accreditation you can check, a named device, a companion plan for heart or brain surgery, a refusal of paid donation. Decoration: a lobby photo, a claim of being the best medical tourism facilitator in India, and a list of countries with no description of your case. The [facilitator guide](/blog/best-medical-tourism-facilitators-india) and the [agency guide](/blog/best-medical-tourism-agency-india) walk through those decorations. The [hospital questions](/blog/how-to-choose-a-hospital-abroad) are what you still ask after you pick a company, because the company is not the surgeon.
