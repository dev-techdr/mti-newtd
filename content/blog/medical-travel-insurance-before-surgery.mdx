<section id="quick-answer" aria-label="Quick answer">

Buy travel insurance only after you have read whether planned treatment abroad is excluded. Many policies cover an unexpected illness on a holiday and refuse the operation you flew to have, and sometimes the complications of that operation. TechDr does not sell insurance and does not choose your policy. The hospital quote and the insurance schedule are different documents. You may need both.

</section>

## What does a typical travel policy refuse?

Elective surgery, including a joint replacement, a cataract, a hair transplant, or a bypass you scheduled. A policy that excludes “planned treatment” will not pay the hospital invoice you already accepted. Read the schedule for complications too. Some policies pay for an emergency that follows elective care. Many do not. Assume they do not until the wording says they do.

A deposit paid to a hospital is not an insurance product. If the visa is refused or the surgeon says you are not fit, the refund terms should be on the hospital letter, not in a travel policy you hoped would cover disappointment.

## Which questions should you send the insurer in writing?

Are complications of a named elective procedure covered, and up to what amount. Is a return to theatre covered. Is an extra ICU stay covered. Is evacuation covered if you are stable enough to move, and who decides. Is a companion’s trip covered if you cannot fly on the booked day. Does the policy require treatment in a named network. Send the procedure name. “Medical tourism” is too vague for an underwriter.

Keep the reply. A phone assurance that is not on the schedule is weak when a claim is refused.

## What costs will you still pay yourself?

The operation, if it is excluded. The flight. The visa fee. A hotel after discharge. Medicines after the package supply ends. A companion’s food. Time off work. The [cost comparison guide](/blog/medical-tourism-cost-calculator) is the place to add those lines to the surgery quote. Insurance is not a reason to skip that sum.

## How does insurance interact with fitness to fly?

Insurers and surgeons can both say you should not board. The surgeon’s clearance decides the medical question. The insurer’s rule decides the money. Flying against advice because a policy will not pay another week of hotel is how people board with a clot, a fever, or an open wound. Read [flying home after surgery](/blog/flying-home-after-surgery) before you buy a non-refundable return.

## What should you carry if you do claim?

The operation note, the discharge summary, the itemised bill, and the insurance policy number. Ask the hospital for invoices in a form a foreign insurer can read. English summaries are part of what TechDr asks partner hospitals to provide. A claim without an itemised bill is slow even when the policy would have paid.

## What TechDr will not do

TechDr will not tell you a policy is “fine” without the schedule, and it will not delay a clinically urgent warning so that a fare stays valid. If you are unwell tonight, use local emergency care. Insurance paperwork can follow. It cannot replace the nearest hospital.
