A liver transplant package for the recipient in India is listed in the 2026 TechDr guide at about USD 28,000–40,000. That figure is an indicative recipient surgery range, not a promise of a donor, a bed, or a survival figure. Living donation is lawful only through the hospital’s committee and the relationship rules then in force. TechDr refuses paid donors. Many families should plan on well over a month in India, and some will be told not to travel.

## What does the price cover, and what does it not?

The guide range of USD 28,000–40,000 is a recipient surgery package. Published comparison bands in the same guide are USD 500,000 and above in the USA and USD 100,000 and above in the UK. Those are planning numbers. A real letter itemises the evaluation, the operation, a stated intensive-care period, the ward stay, and standard medicines in hospital. It does not quietly include a donor who has not been assessed.

The donor’s evaluation and the donor’s surgery are separate clinical events with their own risks. They must be priced honestly. So must the expensive medicines after discharge, the treatment of rejection, a return to theatre, and a longer intensive-care stay. If your quote has one number and no exclusions, it is not a transplant quote.

## Why is the donor the whole pathway?

Most international patients who travel to India for a liver transplant are hoping for a living donor, usually a family member, because a deceased-donor organ is allocated under Indian rules and is not something a foreign patient can purchase. The donor must volunteer, must be fit, and must be accepted by the hospital’s authorisation process. That process can say no. A no is a success of the system, not a failure of the coordinator.

TechDr will not look for a paid donor, will not suggest a stranger presented as a cousin, and will not help anyone rehearse a false relationship. If that is the request, we stop. Organ trade is illegal and it kills donors and recipients in different ways. We would rather lose the enquiry.

## Who can be evaluated as a donor?

The hospital, and the law, decide. Commonly the team looks at a close relative and at whether the relationship can be documented. Documents that do not match, or a story that changes, will stop the case. Blood group, liver size, and the donor’s own health can also stop it, even when the relationship is genuine. A willing spouse or sibling is not automatically a suitable donor. The donor has the right to stop at any point, in private, without the recipient in the room.

You should tell the donor that death and serious complications, while uncommon in careful programmes, are possible. If you cannot say that sentence, you are not ready to ask them.

## How long does the evaluation take?

Plan for repeated blood tests, scans, a heart and lung review, an independent doctor for the donor, and a committee. This is not a long weekend. Families who arrive expecting surgery on day three are the families who feel rushed, and rushed transplant consent is bad consent. Budget a stay that can stretch, and a visa that can cover it. Confirm the visa category on the official portal. A tourist stamp is the wrong tool for this trip.

Some recipients are too sick to fly. Jaundice and a waiting family do not change aeromedical risk. If the physician at home says the patient cannot fly, the evaluation has to happen without a commercial flight, or not at all. TechDr will not book that flight against medical advice.

## What risks should both people understand?

The recipient faces bleeding, graft failure, infection, rejection, bile-duct problems, and a long medicine list. The donor faces bleeding, bile leak, clot, and a recovery that can take months, with time away from work. Reported series from established units describe good survival for selected patients. Those series are not your percentage. Nobody should sell you a survival figure in a chat.

Children and adults with tumours need extra staging before anyone discusses a graft. A tumour that has spread is not made transplantable by a flight.

## What should you send first?

Send the diagnosis, recent blood tests including liver and kidney function, imaging, endoscopy reports, the current medicine list, and a letter from the treating liver doctor. Say who the proposed donor is and how they are related, and send their blood group if you have it. Do not send the donor’s passport as if that were an approval. We will tell you if the file is too incomplete to justify a visa, or if the patient needs care locally first.

Photographs of the hospital you hope to visit are irrelevant at this stage. The labs are the whole conversation.

## How should the family travel?

At least one healthy adult should travel with the recipient, and the donor needs their own support. These may be different people. The donor should not be the recipient’s only attendant, because the donor will be a patient too. Hotels near the hospital matter more than hotels with a view. After discharge, both people need to stay close enough for daily or frequent blood tests. Flying home early to save a hotel bill is a known way to miss rejection.

Money for an extra month is part of the true cost. Read the [cost comparison guide](/blog/medical-tourism-cost-calculator) before you sell property based on the surgery line alone.

## What will the discharge plan include?

Immunosuppression doses, the infection-warning symptoms, wound care, the diet the team wants, and a named doctor at home who has agreed to prescribe and to do blood tests. Generic names of drugs matter, because brand names differ by country. Ask the home doctor, before you leave, whether those drugs are available and affordable. A transplant that you cannot medicate at home is not a completed plan.

Carry papers in the cabin bag. Keep a photo of the medicine chart on two phones.

## What TechDr will and will not do

We will ask an experienced transplant unit at a partner hospital to say whether evaluation is reasonable. We will help with the invitation letter, the practical stay, and interpreters. We will not name a celebrity surgeon, invent a survival rate, or introduce a paid donor. If the committee refuses, we stop. That outcome is better than an illegal operation.

## After a refusal

If the committee or the donor’s own doctor says no, stop. Ask the recipient’s liver doctor at home what the next lawful option is, including care without a transplant. Do not contact a second coordinator to shop for a more flexible committee. Flexibility in this setting is a warning. Keep the evaluation records. They may spare the donor a repeated scan later, and they show that you tried the legal route.

A kidney transplant raises the same donation rules even though the operation and the USD 13,000–18,000 recipient range are different. The principle does not change with the organ. Paid donation is refused there too.
