Compare treatment in India with treatment at home by adding the full trip, not by subtracting a headline surgery price. Include flights, visas, a companion, the hotel, extra nights if recovery slips, medicines after discharge, and lost income. The 2026 TechDr guide often shows a large gap on the surgery line. The gap shrinks, and should shrink, once the trip is honest. It does not become a guarantee of savings.

</section>

## Why do headline savings mislead?

A page that leads with a savings percentage is talking about the difference between two surgery ranges, usually before travel. For a single knee, the 2026 guide lists USD 4,500–7,000 in India, USD 35,000–50,000 in the USA, and USD 18,000–25,000 in the UK. The midpoint gap is large. A couple of return flights, three weeks of hotels, and a companion’s unpaid leave do not erase it for many people. They do erase it for a cheap operation with an expensive route, or for a person whose home care is already insured.

If your insurer covers the operation at home with a small copay, medical travel can cost you more, not less. Run that sum first. TechDr is not offended if the answer is to stay home.

## What belongs in the India column?

Put the written surgical quote, not the blog range. Add the visa fees for each traveller, return flights, a hotel for the nights outside hospital, food, local travel, a SIM, and a buffer of extra nights. Add discharge medicines. Add a second ticket change in case the surgeon delays the flight. Add travel insurance only if the policy actually covers complications of planned surgery. Many cheap policies exclude that. An excluded policy is not a safety line. It is a decoration.

Add lost income for the patient and the companion. A month of unpaid leave can be worth more than the implant.

## What belongs in the home column?

Put the real price you would pay, not a chargemaster figure you would never be billed. If you have insurance, use the deductible and copay. If you are paying cash, ask for a packaged cash price that includes the same items as the Indian quote: surgeon, anaesthesia, device, ward, and a named ICU allowance. A home quote for “surgeon only” against an Indian quote for “everything in hospital” is a false comparison.

Add time off work at home too. Recovery does not vanish because the operating theatre was local. The difference is the extra days of travel and the extra risk of being far away if something goes wrong.

## How do you treat open-ended foreign prices?

Some rows in the guide, such as a kidney or liver transplant in the USA, are shown as “USD 300,000+” or “USD 500,000+” because public ranges do not have a tidy ceiling. Do not pretend those plus signs are exact. Use them as a floor for illustration. Your home transplant centre, if you have access to one, can give a more relevant figure. Insurance changes that figure more than geography does.

The India ranges are also estimates. A complication can double a stay. The right-hand column needs a buffer, and so does the left.

## A simple way to do the arithmetic

Write two totals. India total equals quote plus travel plus lodging plus medicines plus lost income plus a buffer you choose, such as two extra weeks for major surgery. Home total equals your real out-of-pocket cost plus lost income at home. Subtract. If the India total is lower, you have a financial reason to look further. You do not yet have a medical reason. The medical reason is a team that has read your reports and a plan you understand.

If the India total is only slightly lower, the extra risk of distance may not be worth it. Distance is a cost even when it is not a line on the invoice. A clot in a foreign hotel is not priced in the knee package.

## Which procedures leave more room for travel costs?

A cataract at USD 600–1,500 per eye, or a dental implant at USD 500–900 per tooth, can be wiped out by a long-haul flight for one tooth or one eye. People still travel when several implants or both eyes are planned, or when they are already visiting family. A bypass listed at USD 5,000–8,500 against a US range of USD 70,000–150,000 leaves more room for flights, and still needs the angiogram to justify the trip. A health check at USD 150–400 is not a reason to fly by itself unless you are already in India.

Use the [cost estimator](/get-a-free-quote) as a sketch, then replace the sketch with a written quote. The estimator uses guide midpoints. It is not your bill.

## What savings should you refuse?

Refuse a saving that comes from a paid organ donor, a shared medicine, an unaccredited theatre, or a quote that excludes ICU for an operation that sometimes needs ICU. Refuse a saving that requires you to fly while unstable. Those are not savings. They are transfers of risk onto you.

Also refuse a comparison that ignores a complication fund. If you cannot afford an extra ICU week, you cannot afford the operation abroad, even when the first invoice looks small.

## How do companions change the sum?

One companion is the minimum we suggest for any admission. Their flight, visa, food, and leave are part of the medical plan. Two companions may be necessary for a child or a transplant, because the donor and the recipient cannot look after each other. Put those fares in the spreadsheet before you tell relatives the trip is cheap.

A companion who speaks the language and can stay the whole time is worth more than a slightly cheaper ticket on a worse date.

## How to keep the comparison honest

Use one currency, dated in 2026, and write the source of each number. Guide ranges come from the TechDr cost table. Home numbers should come from your insurer or your hospital. Update the India side when the written quote arrives, and throw the blog range away for decision-making. Keep the blog range only as a check that the quote is not a different order of magnitude.

Then decide with a clinician, not with the spreadsheet alone. A lower total that is the wrong operation is still the wrong operation.

## Worked shape, not a personal total

Imagine a single knee quoted at USD 6,000, two return fares at a price you look up this week, three weeks of a modest hotel, visas, and a medicine supply. Write those numbers yourself. Then imagine your home out-of-pocket cost. The blog cannot know your fare or your deductible, so it will not pretend to finish the sum. What it can say is that forgetting the companion’s fare is the usual error, and that a buffer of extra nights belongs in the India column for any admission that can slip.

Do the same exercise for a smaller procedure before you book. A dental implant at USD 500–900 per tooth plus a long-haul flight for one tooth is often a poor exchange. Five teeth, or a trip you are already making, can look different. The arithmetic is the point. The brochure percentage is not.
