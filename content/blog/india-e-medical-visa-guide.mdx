An India e-Medical Visa is an electronic visa category for people travelling for treatment, applied for online before the flight. A hospital invitation letter supports the file. It is not the visa, and it does not guarantee approval. A companion may need a medical attendant visa. Rules, fees, eligible countries, length of stay, and entries change. Confirm every step on the official Indian visa portal before you pay or book tickets.

</section>

## What is the e-Medical Visa?

India distinguishes a medical visit from tourism. The e-Medical Visa is the online route many international patients use when their nationality is eligible. You apply before travel, upload documents, pay the fee shown on the official portal, and wait for a decision. You should not buy a non-refundable ticket until you know that decision. TechDr can coordinate the hospital letter that the application asks for. TechDr cannot issue the visa and cannot see the government’s decision before you do.

There is a separate idea of a medical attendant visa for a person travelling with the patient. Couples sometimes assume one visa covers both. It does not. Each person needs their own application, their own passport, and their own fee. Children need their own applications too.

## Who should apply, and who should not rely on this guide?

Eligibility depends on nationality, the purpose of the trip, and the rules in force on the day you apply. Some nationalities use a different channel, such as a sticker visa through a mission, or an e-visa category with a different name. This page cannot list a permanent eligible-country table, because that table is the government’s and it changes. If your passport country is not offered the medical e-visa, do not try to travel on a tourist visa and “explain at the hospital”. The hospital may treat you, and the immigration officer may still refuse you.

People who already hold a valid Indian visa of another type should ask the official portal whether that visa allows medical treatment. Do not stack applications without reading the instruction for your nationality.

## Which documents do patients usually prepare?

You will typically need a passport that meets the validity the portal states, a digital photo in the size the portal states, copies of medical reports that show why you are travelling, and a letter from the hospital you plan to visit. The letter should name you, the hospital, the kind of treatment, and a planned date window. It should not invent a diagnosis you do not have. TechDr asks the partner hospital for this letter after a clinician has seen enough of your file to say the trip is plausible.

Proof of funds and a return ticket are sometimes requested. A companion’s passport and relationship, where the form asks, should match the documents you upload. Blurry photos and mismatched names are ordinary reasons for delay. Use the same spelling as the passport, including the order of names.

## How does the application usually run?

Create the application on the official Indian visa website only. Ignore links sent by strangers. Fill the form slowly. The medical category is not the tourist category. Upload the hospital letter and the reports the form requests, within the file-size limit. Pay the fee the portal shows. Save the application number. Some applicants must carry a printed copy. The portal will say so.

Processing time is not a promise. It can be a few days and it can be longer if a document is queried. Apply as soon as you have a real hospital letter, and keep the travel dates flexible. If the portal asks a question, answer it. Do not send a second application unless the portal tells you to.

## What does the hospital invitation letter do?

The letter tells the visa officer that a hospital in India is willing to assess or treat you on particular dates. It is evidence of purpose. It is not permission to enter India, not a booking of a surgeon, and not a fixed price. If your reports change, or the surgeon later says you are not fit to travel, the letter can be withdrawn or updated. Using an old letter for a different operation is a good way to create a problem at the airport.

TechDr will not provide a letter for paid organ donation, for a fabricated illness, or for someone who needs emergency care in their own city. We also will not backdate a letter so that a ticket bought too early looks planned.

## How long is the visa valid, and how many entries?

Validity, the number of entries, and the maximum stay are printed on the grant and in the current public rules. They are not something a coordinator should invent in a chat. Read the grant before you design a trip that needs a second visit, for example a dental implant that is restored months later, or a cancer plan with a later cycle. If one entry is all you have, a second trip needs a new application.

Overstaying is an immigration problem, not a medical one. If recovery takes longer than the stay you were given, talk to the official authorities early. Do not assume a hospital can extend a visa because you are still a patient.

## How should the visa date and the surgery date fit together?

Work backwards from a flexible medical window. Get the clinical opinion and the invitation letter first. Apply for the visa. When it is granted, book flights that leave enough days before admission for a repeat blood test and a physician’s review, and enough days after surgery for the stay the procedure page describes. A knee often needs two to three weeks in India. A transplant needs much longer. A health check may need only a few days. The visa must cover that whole period, not only the night of surgery.

Build a buffer. A delayed scan or a low blood count can move the operation by several days. A ticket with no change option is a clinical risk disguised as a bargain.

## What should companions know?

The attendant is there to help you wash, walk, and speak to the team. They should apply as an attendant, stay in the same city, and understand they are not a second patient unless they have their own medical file. Hospitals can usually give an attendant a place to rest. That place may be a chair or a paid extra bed. Ask which one is in the quote.

If your attendant must fly home earlier than you, plan a replacement or accept that you will be alone for wound checks. For major surgery, being alone is a bad plan.

## What if the visa is refused?

A refusal is the government’s decision. TechDr cannot overturn it. You can read the reason, correct a document if the rules allow a fresh application, and only then think about travel again. Do not fly to a third country to “try a different airport” on advice from a forum. If you had paid a hospital deposit, use the written refund rule you agreed before you paid. A deposit should say what happens on visa refusal.

Keep planning your care at home while you wait. A visa delay is not a reason to stop medicines.

## Where should you confirm the rules?

Confirm eligibility, fees, photo rules, processing, and stay length on the official Indian visa portal, and if your country uses a mission, on that mission’s instructions.
