<section id="quick-answer" aria-label="Quick answer">

Before you fly home, collect the operation note, the implant or device stickers, the medicine list with doses and stop dates, the discharge summary in English, and the latest blood tests or imaging reports. Carry them in your cabin bag, not in checked luggage. A doctor at home cannot continue a stent, a valve, a transplant, or a joint replacement from memory. TechDr asks partner hospitals for an English summary for that reason.

</section>

## Which papers matter for almost every operation?

The discharge summary: what was done, the date, the findings, and the warnings. The medicine chart: drug, dose, time of day, and how long to continue. Allergies. The wound-care instructions. The fitness-to-fly note if one was issued. A contact at the hospital for the first weeks. Photos of the wound on the day of discharge help if a nurse at home asks what “normal” looked like. They do not replace the summary.

## What do joints, hearts, eyes, and implants add?

Joint replacements need the implant stickers and the bearing that was used. Heart valves need the valve type and the blood-thinner plan. Stents need the stent name and the antiplatelet course. You must not stop those tablets early. Spine implants need the card you may be asked to show at a metal detector. Cochlear implants need the manufacturer and model so an audiologist can programme the processor. Dental implants need the fixture details so a crown can be made on the right connection. Cataract patients need the lens model and the drop chart. Hair transplant patients need the graft count and the wash instructions.

## What do cancer and transplant patients add?

Pathology, when it is ready, and a clear statement if it is still pending. A pending report is a reason to know who will send it, not a reason to invent a stage. Transplant patients need the immunosuppression doses and the blood-test schedule. Stopping those drugs because you feel well is dangerous. Bone marrow transplant patients need the fever instructions in writing. A surgery package was never a year of cancer care. The [cancer guide](/blog/cancer-treatment-in-india) explains how to split what was done in India from what continues at home.

## How should you pack them?

Paper copies and a folder you can open without a password. A scan in your email as backup. Checked bags go missing. If you are travelling with a companion, give them a second copy. Translate nothing away: keep the English original your surgeon signed, and add a translation beside it if your home doctor needs one.

## Who should see them, and when?

The clinician who will continue your care, soon after landing, with a sooner visit if the discharge letter says so. A heart patient should not wait a month for a first local review. Sudden chest pain, breathlessness, a hot draining wound, a swollen calf, confusion, or a fever after a transplant is emergency care. Do not wait for an email reply from India for those symptoms. The [flying home guide](/blog/flying-home-after-surgery) covers the flight itself.

This is a packing list for records, not a personal discharge plan. Your letter overrides a general article. Use the [free quote form](/get-a-free-quote) if you are still before travel, and ask for the English summary to be promised in the quote, not requested on the morning you leave.
