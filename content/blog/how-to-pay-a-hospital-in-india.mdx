<section id="quick-answer" aria-label="Quick answer">

Pay the hospital named on the written quote, against an invoice that matches that quote. Do not transfer a surgery fee to a personal account, a private wallet, or a stranger who offers to “hold a bed”. A deposit, if one is asked before the visa, should be written, small relative to the total, and refundable if the visa is refused or the surgeon later says you are not fit to travel. TechDr does not ask you to pay an individual’s private account.

</section>

## What should an invoice show?

The hospital’s legal name, the operation, the currency, what the amount covers, and what it does not cover. Match it to the quote line by line: implant or device, nights, ICU cap, and exclusions. If the invoice is a round number and the quote was a list, ask for the list to be attached. A receipt that says “package” is weak when you need to show an insurer or an embassy what you bought.

## What is a reasonable deposit?

Enough for the hospital to schedule, not the entire stay in advance, and not before you have a quote you understand. Written refund rules matter more than a verbal promise in a chat. If the authorisation committee refuses a transplant, or the surgeon cancels because of a new heart problem on your tests, the deposit terms should say what comes back. Those terms belong in the letter before you send money.

## Which payments should you refuse?

A request to split the fee so that part goes to a coordinator’s personal account. A discount for cash that bypasses the invoice. A change of beneficiary the day before theatre. A request for your card PIN, a one-time password, or remote access to your banking app. Those are not hospital billing. Stop and use the contact details on the [contact page](/contact), not a new number that appeared in a message.

Currency exchange and bank fees are yours to expect. Ask which currency the hospital receives and who pays the transfer charge. A quote in US dollars that is collected in another currency needs the rate in writing.

## How do extra nights get paid?

The quote should already show the price of one extra ward day and one extra ICU day. Paying that rate for a fever or a slow recovery is normal. Discovering the rate only after discharge is not. Complications and a return to theatre follow the exclusion list. Read it before you travel. The [package guide](/blog/whats-included-in-a-medical-tourism-package) is the checklist.

## What records do you keep?

The quote, the invoice, the transfer confirmation, and the final itemised bill. Carry copies in your cabin bag with the operation note. The [records guide](/blog/records-to-carry-home-after-surgery) lists the clinical papers. The bank papers matter if a deposit is disputed or an insurer asks what you paid.
