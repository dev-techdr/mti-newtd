A free second opinion from TechDr is a written view from a partner hospital on the reports you send, plus an indicative estimate if treatment in India is even appropriate. It is not a diagnosis from a chat screenshot, not an emergency service, and not a booking. Send the real files. Most planned cases receive that written view within about 48 hours after the file is complete. Incomplete files wait. Urgent symptoms belong in a local emergency department.

## What is a second opinion in this context?

It is a clinician at an accredited partner hospital looking at your history and your images and saying, in writing, whether the proposed operation makes sense, what else they would want to know, and what an indicative cost would be if you travelled. TechDr coordinates the file. We do not replace the clinician, and we do not add a success percentage the clinician did not write.

You can want a second opinion and still stay at home for treatment. A good opinion sometimes says “do this locally” or “you do not need surgery”. That is a completed request, not a failed sale.

## What should you send?

Send the question in one sentence. “Do I need a knee replacement?” is usable. “Please help” is not. Then send the reports that answer it: imaging files, not only a photo of a screen if you can avoid it; pathology if this is cancer; blood tests; previous operation notes; and a medicine list including allergies. Name your country, your age, and whether you can travel with a companion. Say if you are pregnant or if you have had a recent heart attack. Those facts change whether a flight is even discussable.

Use the [quote form](/get-a-free-quote). Any file type is accepted, up to 10 files and 10 MB each. If a disc will not upload, say so, and send the written report first. A hospital may still need the images before a final view.

## What should you leave out?

Do not send another person’s reports. Do not send a demand for a named celebrity doctor. Do not send a request to invent a diagnosis for a visa. Do not send payment card details in the upload. The form asks for contact details so a coordinator can reply. The clinical file is the reports.

If the question is really “can you find a cheaper donor?”, stop. We will not issue an opinion that assists organ trade.

## How fast is “48 hours”?

The clock starts when the file is readable, the consent box is ticked, and the question is clear. A weekend, a missing scan, or a question that needs a tumour board is slower, and someone should tell you that rather than invent a deadline. Forty-eight hours is the target for a planned case with a complete file. It is not a promise that a transplant committee will meet on that timetable. Committees take longer. We would rather say so.

You will not get a useful opinion in the time it takes to type a text message. Anyone offering that speed has not looked at the images.

## What will the reply contain?

Expect a statement of what the clinician understood, what they would advise as the next step, which extra tests matter, whether travel is reasonable, and a cost range if a procedure is appropriate. The range should match the kind of estimate on our procedure pages, or explain why your case is outside it. It should name exclusions in ordinary language. It should not say “promised success”.

If the clinician disagrees with a plan you were given at home, you still decide with your own doctor. Two opinions are an input. They are not a vote you must obey.

## What will the reply not contain?

It will not clear you to fly if you are acutely ill. It will not prescribe a controlled medicine by email for you to start tonight. It will not confirm a cancer stage from a paragraph with no pathology. It will not hold a bed. A bed is a later step, after you accept a plan and the visa is real.

It will also not be a public review of a named doctor. We do not publish those names on the website, and a second opinion is not a licence to post someone’s report on social media. The file is confidential.

## How do you use the opinion well?

Read it with a clinician who knows you, or at least with a family member who will ask what you missed. Write down what would change your mind. If the opinion says you need a test first, get the test before you price the flights. If it says surgery is reasonable, move to a formal quote, a visa letter, and the stay length on the procedure page. If it says do not travel, believe it the first time. Shopping the same file until someone says yes is how people buy the wrong operation.

Keep the PDF. Your home doctor may want it. The Indian team will want it too if you later arrive, so the story stays stable.

## What if you disagree or you are worse?

Reply with the specific sentence you think is wrong and the report that supports you. A correction is more useful than a second upload of the same scan. If your symptoms change (chest pain, bleeding, a new weakness, a fever), stop the email thread and get local emergency care. Tell the coordinator afterwards so the opinion is not used as if you were still stable.

You can ask for your file to be deleted under the [privacy policy](/privacy-policy). Deletion does not recall a letter a hospital has already stored under its own rules.

## How the opinion relates to price

If travel is reasonable, the letter should sit next to the 2026 guide range for that procedure and say why your case is inside it or outside it. A single knee often starts from USD 4,500–7,000. A bypass often starts from USD 5,000–8,500. A figure far below the guide, with no explanation, is a reason to ask what was removed from the bundle, not a reason to celebrate. A figure far above it may be honest if you need ICU, a revision implant, or a longer stay. The opinion should make that reason visible.

You do not pay for this first written view. You also should not be pushed to pay a surgical deposit inside the same email. Read the view, talk to your own doctor, and only then decide whether a visa letter is worth requesting. If two partner units disagree, send both letters home and keep both, rather than picking the easier one.
