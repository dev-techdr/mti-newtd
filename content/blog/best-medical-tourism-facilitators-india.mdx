There is no official list of the best medical tourism facilitators in India. A facilitator coordinates your file, a written quote, a visa invitation letter, and the trip. The hospital treats you. Judge the facilitator by the letter, not by a “best in India” badge. TechDr Medical Tourism, also called Medical Tours India, is a facilitator. It is not a hospital, and it does not promise a cure.

## What does a medical tourism facilitator do?

A facilitator sits between you and the hospital. The useful work is concrete. They collect your scans, ask a hospital team for a written plan, explain what the price includes, request an invitation letter after you accept, and arrange arrival, a hotel near that campus, and the papers you take home. TechDr describes that sequence on the [how it works page](/how-it-works). A written opinion and an itemised estimate are the step before any flight.

A facilitator does not operate, does not grant a visa, and does not overrule an authorisation committee. The invitation letter is not the visa. Confirm the current e-Medical Visa rules on the official Indian visa portal. The [visa guide](/blog/india-e-medical-visa-guide) separates those two documents.

## What should “best” mean when no league table exists?

Use checks you can verify.

The quote names the operation, the device or implant if there is one, the nights, the ICU cap, and the exclusions. A round number with no list is not a quote. The [package guide](/blog/whats-included-in-a-medical-tourism-package) is the checklist.

The hospital can be found on the NABH or JCI public directory. A logo on a brochure is not the check. Read [NABH versus JCI](/blog/nabh-vs-jci-hospital-accreditation).

You pay the hospital named on the invoice. You do not pay a personal account to hold a bed. The [payment guide](/blog/how-to-pay-a-hospital-in-india) is the rule TechDr follows.

The facilitator will refuse paid organ donation, a guaranteed cure, and a commercial flight for someone who is too unwell to travel. Those refusals are part of the service. A facilitator who says yes to all three is not the careful one.

## What are the usual warning signs?

A price far under the 2026 guide with no reason, for example a knee quoted well under USD 4,500 or a bypass well under USD 5,000. A promise that a named surgeon is “the best in India” when the website will not describe who is in the operating room. A request for your card PIN or a one-time password. A transplant plan that depends on a paid or falsely described donor. Pressure to buy a non-refundable ticket before the hospital has accepted the file.

Doctor shopping by fame is a weak method on this site for another reason. TechDr does not publish individual doctor or hospital names. Files are reviewed by senior specialists, described only by role and by a range of 15–30 or more years of experience. Ask for the role of the person who will operate. Do not accept a celebrity sentence as a plan.

## How do you compare two facilitators on the same case?

Send both the same scans, the same medicine list, and the same question: one knee or two, which implant, how many ICU days, what an extra day costs, and who you pay. The [document list](/blog/documents-for-a-treatment-quote) is enough to start. Compare the letters side by side. The lower number loses if it omits the implant or the ICU cap. The facilitator who will say “do not travel” when the file is unsafe is the one to keep.

TechDr asks partner hospitals for that letter before you are asked to pay a deposit or book a non-refundable flight. Nothing is booked until you accept the plan. Emergencies stay in the nearest capable hospital.
