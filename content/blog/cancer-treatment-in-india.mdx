Cancer treatment in India for international patients is usually a defined episode, such as an operation or a planning visit, not a promise to replace your whole oncology team. Surgery packages in the 2026 guide, such as about USD 4,000–7,000 for breast cancer surgery or USD 6,500–10,000 for brain tumour surgery, cover that episode’s starting estimate. Drug therapy and radiotherapy are separate plans. TechDr does not guarantee remission.

</section>

## What can reasonably be planned as a trip?

An operation with a clear goal, a staging visit, a second opinion on scans, or a short block of treatment that a home oncologist has agreed to continue. Examples include breast cancer surgery, some head and neck operations, a brain tumour operation when the scans support it, or a urology operation. A full body check is not cancer treatment. It is a screen, and a screen that finds something still needs a proper diagnosis at a pace that may not match a holiday visa.

What often should not be a trip is an emergency, a cancer that is changing week by week, or a drug that you cannot obtain at home after the first cycle. Starting a medicine you cannot continue is harmful, not helpful.

## Why must surgery, drugs, and radiotherapy be priced apart?

They are different treatments. A surgery quote that quietly implies chemotherapy is included will not survive contact with a pharmacy. Drugs vary with the diagnosis, the receptor status, and the protocol. Radiotherapy depends on the number of sessions and the technique. Ask for three quotes if you might need three treatments, and ask which of them your doctor at home can take over.

The breast surgery range of USD 4,000–7,000 and the brain tumour surgery range of USD 6,500–10,000 are indicative theatre-and-stay estimates. They are not the cost of a year of cancer care. Reconstruction, special implants, molecular drugs, and a long intensive-care stay sit outside those bands unless the letter says otherwise.

## What should you send for an opinion?

The pathology report, not just the word “cancer”. The imaging, on disc or as files a radiologist can open. Previous operations and the drug names and dates. A letter from your oncologist stating the question you want answered. “Is surgery possible?” is a better question than “what is the named hospital?”. If the pathology was done elsewhere, the Indian team may want the slides reviewed. That review takes days. Build the days in.

Do not send only a WhatsApp photo of a report if you can send the file. Do send the photo if that is all you have, and expect follow-up questions.

## How do you keep your home oncologist in the plan?

Write to them before you fly. Ask whether they will resume care, prescribe the next cycle, and accept the Indian pathology. Some will. Some will want to repeat tests. Their answer changes whether the trip is wise. A second opinion that your own team will not read is an expensive PDF.

Ask the Indian team for a discharge summary a home oncologist can use: the operation, the final pathology when it is ready, the drugs given, and the recommended next step. Final pathology can take days. Do not fly out the morning after surgery if the plan depends on that report and the visa allows you to wait.

## What are the limits of a surgical trip?

Surgery removes or diagnoses. It does not, by itself, finish most cancers. You may need drugs, radiation, or both, over months. You may need a second operation. Reported ranges from trials are about groups, not about you. Nobody on a medical-travel website can honestly say “stage 3 is cured in India”. If you read that sentence, you are in the wrong queue.

Infection, bleeding, and clots are the early risks, as with other major surgery. The cancer-specific risk is a plan that stops halfway. Budget the halfway.

## How long might you stay?

It depends on the operation. A straightforward breast cancer operation may be a shorter admission than a brain tumour operation, and both need a stay long enough for the first complications to show and for the summary to be written. Radiotherapy, if you choose to have it in India, can mean daily visits for weeks. That is a different visa and hotel plan from a five-night admission. Confirm the visa on the official portal and match it to the real calendar, not to the advertisement.

A companion should stay. Cancer admissions are a bad time to be alone in a city you do not know.

## What will TechDr not arrange?

We will not arrange an unproven therapy sold as a cure, a guarantee of shrinkage, or a reason to stop a conventional plan your oncologist still recommends. We will not hide a stage to make a quote look simpler. We will ask a partner unit for an opinion on the file you actually have. Sometimes the opinion is that surgery is not the right next step. That answer saves you a flight.

Experimental drugs belong in a trial with ethics oversight, not in a package holiday. Ask whether a suggested drug is licensed and available at home.

## How should you compare cost with home?

Use the method in the [cost comparison guide](/blog/medical-tourism-cost-calculator). Put the episode quote, the flights, the extra weeks, and the drugs you will buy later. Compare with what your insurer would pay at home. A cash price in India can be lower and still be the wrong choice if it breaks a funded pathway at home. Bring both numbers to your oncologist.

## Questions worth sending with the scans

Ask whether the goal is cure, a longer period of control, or relief of a specific symptom such as pain or blockage. Those are different operations. Ask what the team would do if the scans on arrival look worse than the scans you sent. Ask which symptoms during your stay should bring you back at night. Ask how pain medicines will be handed to your doctor at home, because a gap of even a few days matters.

If you are comparing a cash price with insured care at home, write both numbers down before you leave a pathway you cannot rejoin. A lower surgical invoice is not a saving if it costs you radiotherapy that was already authorised in your own city. Families sometimes discover that after the operation, when the useful decision has already been spent. The [safety guide](/blog/is-medical-treatment-in-india-safe) applies here with extra force: accreditation, a named night route, and a companion are the minimum. Carry the pathology slides if the lab will release them, because a repeat read can change an operation that was planned from a summary alone. Ask for that possibility before you book a non-refundable fare.
