Medical treatment in India can be safe for planned care when the hospital is accredited, the surgeon’s plan matches your reports, and you are well enough to fly. Safety is not a country slogan and it is not a star rating. It is accreditation you can check, a written quote, a companion, and a refusal to travel while you are unstable. ## Is treatment in India safe for someone travelling from abroad?

It can be, for the right operation and the right person. India has hospitals that hold NABH accreditation, the national hospital standard, and some that also hold JCI accreditation, an international hospital standard. Those marks say the organisation has been reviewed for systems such as infection control, consent, and record-keeping. They do not say that every operation in that building will go well, and they do not replace a surgeon reading your own scans.

The unsafe version of medical travel is the opposite: a price on a chat app, a hospital you cannot name, a departure while you still have chest pain or a fever, and no one who can stay with you. TechDr will not build that trip. If a case is an emergency, the right hospital is the nearest one at home.

## What should you check before you trust a hospital?

Ask for the hospital’s accreditation and confirm it on the accreditor’s own public list, not on a screenshot in a brochure. Ask who will actually operate, and whether a senior clinician will be present rather than only “available”. Ask which intensive-care beds are reserved for your type of surgery and what happens if those beds are full on your date. Ask how the hospital handles a complication at night, and which number you call from the hotel.

Ask for English discharge papers, or papers in a language your doctor at home can use. A successful operation that nobody at home can follow is an incomplete plan. Ask whether the hospital has treated people from your country before. Experience with visas and attendants is not the same as surgical skill, but it does tell you whether the coordinator has done this more than once.

## How do you judge the clinician without a famous name?

TechDr does not publish doctors’ names on this website. You should still receive, in the written plan, the seniority of the person who will operate and the type of unit: for example a cardiac theatre team, a joint-replacement service, or a transplant committee. Fifteen to thirty years of specialist practice is the level we ask partner hospitals to offer for major surgery. Years alone are not magic. Volume of the specific operation matters more than a general reputation.

You are allowed to ask how many of this exact procedure the unit did in the last year, and what they do when a case is more complex than expected. A team that says “we do everything” without limits is a weaker sign than a team that tells you when they would refer you on.

## What does a safe quote look like?

A safe quote is boring. It names the procedure, the implant or device if there is one, the number of ward days, the intensive-care cap, the exclusions, and the price of an extra day. It says whether blood tests, the first medicines, and airport transport are inside the figure. It is issued by the hospital or by TechDr passing on the hospital’s figure, and it matches the reports you sent.

A safe quote also says no. Paid organ donation is refused. A guarantee of cure is refused. A request to hide a diagnosis from a visa form is refused. If someone offers those things at a lower price, leave the conversation.

## When is it unsafe to fly for treatment?

Do not fly with new chest pain, breathlessness at rest, uncontrolled bleeding, a high fever, a new stroke, or a pregnancy complication. Do not fly soon after a heart attack or a major clot because a website said the surgery is cheaper next week. Cabin pressure, sitting still, and distance from an operating theatre are real risks. A physician who knows your history should agree that you are fit to fly before you book.

Children, older adults, and people with significant heart, lung, or kidney disease need a longer checklist. The cheapest date is the wrong date if the escort cannot come, or if the medicines you must not stop have not been planned.

## What is your role in staying safe?

Bring a complete medicine list, allergies, and previous operation notes. Stop smoking if you can, because wounds and clots care about that more than about the country. Control blood sugar if you have diabetes, with your home doctor’s help, before you travel. Tell the team about herbal products and blood thinners. Bring a companion who can stay for the whole admission, not only for the flight.

After surgery, use the hospital’s emergency route for fever, calf swelling, chest pain, sudden breathlessness, or a wound that opens. Do not wait for a coordinator’s office hours. When you fly home, carry the operation note and the implant stickers in the cabin, and see your own doctor within the time the discharge letter specifies.

## How should you think about infection and intensive care?

Every operation has an infection risk. Accreditation requires hospitals to measure and act on infections, but it does not make the risk zero. You lower it by treating infections before travel, by washing, and by not pressuring a team to operate through a dirty wound. Ask whether the quote includes a return to theatre for bleeding or infection in the first days, or whether that is a separate bill. You should know the answer before you consent.

Intensive care is where the safety of major heart, transplant, and cancer surgery actually lives. A package that includes “ICU if needed” without a day cap is vague. A package that includes a named number of ICU days, and a daily price after that, is something you can plan. If your heart or lungs are already fragile, ask for a physician’s view before you leave home, not after the anaesthetic assessment on the night before surgery.

## What about language, food, and consent?

Consent in a language you do not understand is not informed consent. TechDr can arrange interpreters for the languages we support, and you should insist on an interpreter for the consent conversation if you need one. Sign only what has been explained. Take a copy.

Hospitals used by international patients can usually arrange halal food and a place to pray, but you should request both before arrival rather than assume every campus has the same setup. Cultural care does not make the surgery safer. It does make it more likely you will eat, rest, and ask questions.

## How does follow-up protect you after you leave?

The risky week is sometimes the week after you land at home, when a clot or a wound infection appears far from the operating team. Before you fly, you should have a named doctor at home who has agreed to see you, a medicine list they can prescribe, and a way to reach the operating team for a wound photo or an urgent question. TechDr’s coordinator can help pass that message. The coordinator is not a substitute for emergency care.

A video review is reasonable for a healing wound and a medicine question. It is not reasonable for chest pain or a fever with shivering. Use local emergency services for those.
